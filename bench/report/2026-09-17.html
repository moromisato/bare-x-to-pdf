How the score works

Each page from both engines is rasterised at 72 dpi. Ink IoU counts the dark pixels of the reference and of ours and divides their overlap by their union, so a paragraph shifted by one line scores near zero even though a reader would not mind. It is a harsh, layout-first metric: 85% and above is indistinguishable at reading size, 60 to 85% is the same layout with glyphs a fraction of a point off, below 60% something sits in a different place. Similarity is the mean pixel difference and is above 95% for every case, which is why the report leans on IoU.

Text extraction

A PDF that looks right but yields garbage when copied or indexed is not good enough, so both engines' PDFs go through the same extractor, bare-pdfium's extractText, and the result is checked against the words of the source document. The source words come from the document XML itself (text runs of DOCX, ODT, FODT, PPTX and XLSX), so the check is independent of both converters. DOC files have no cheap independent extractor and are compared engine against engine only.

Recall is the share of source words present in the PDF text. Order is the longest common word sequence with the source, so text that comes out in a different reading order loses points even when every word is there. Precision is the share of extracted words that exist in the source; it drops for generated text such as list labels, page numbers and formatted numbers, which is expected and affects both engines alike. Agreement is the word-sequence overlap between the two engines' texts. Words are compared after Unicode normalisation, case-folded, with hyphen variants unified.

Where the two differ, the causes are concrete. When a numbered list's label runs past its tab stop, both engines place the text at the next stop, which can leave no gap, so 3.2.1 xxxx comes back as 3.2.1xxxx from either PDF; we tried forcing a gap and rejected it because the shifted text changed where lines wrap. LibreOffice's WordArt is drawn as curves with no text layer, so the warped slide yields no words at all, and its bullet slides lose a few words to the same label gluing. In the number formats sheet our formatted values match the source more often. Extraction itself takes longer on our PDFs: 113 ms for the corpus against 42 ms, because Typst positions glyphs individually and PDFium has more text objects to walk.

Next improvements

1

Glyph placement parityfidelity, small

Checked against the reference PDFs: the per-glyph advances were never the problem, they agree to within 0.2 pt over a whole line. The offsets were two constants, now emulated: Writer paints every text line 2 twips (0.1 pt) right of its layout position, and Word tables start half a left-border width further right. Text-heavy DOCX, DOC and ODT cases gained 5 to 22 points. What remains is Writer flooring cumulative glyph positions to whole twips, about 0.1 twip per glyph, which needs a patched typst-layout and is worth at most a few points on long lines.

2

Embedded fontsfidelity

DOCX and PPTX can carry their fonts. Loading them into the layout engine would make documents with non-standard fonts match Word rather than fall back to the bundled families.

3

Wrap refinementsfidelity, DOCX

Text now wraps beside a picture for its height in the anchoring paragraph. Pictures taller than that paragraph should also narrow the following paragraphs, and pictures wider than half the text area should wrap tightly instead of pushing text below.

4

Smaller addonsize

Checked: the shipped addon carried a 5 MB symbol table; stripping it in the build takes darwin-arm64 from 33 MB to 26.5 MB, 40 MB with PDFium and fonts. Typst has no feature flags for math, bibliography, code highlighting, PDF embedding or WebAssembly plugins, so dropping them means a patched typst-library (roughly 5 MB more). pdfium-binaries publishes no static libraries; since PDFium is only needed for PDF input, shipping it as an optional package is the larger remaining lever.

Method

  • Corpus: 42 documents in bench/corpus/: real samples, a chart workbook, plus feature fixtures from LibreOffice's test suites (numbering, headers and footers, anchored pictures, footnotes, section breaks, borders, frames, columns, number formats).
  • Reference: bare-collabora 0.1.2 (darwin-arm64) converting the same files in the same process as the benchmark harness, bare bench/run.js.
  • Rasterisation: bare-pdfium at 72 dpi for both PDFs; the two PDF-to-DOCX cases are re-rendered by bare-collabora and compared with the original PDF.
  • Text: bare bench/text.js extracts both PDFs with bare-pdfium 1.2.0 and reads the source words from the document XML with a small zip reader in bench/lib/source-text.js; metrics in bench/lib/text-metrics.js.
  • Timing: bare scripts/timing.js ours|collabora <files> in separate processes; peak memory from /usr/bin/time -l.
  • Sizes: du of the darwin-arm64 prebuild directories.
